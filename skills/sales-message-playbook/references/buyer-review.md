# The buyer review (the 8.5/10 quality gate)

Run this on every draft before delivery. It is structured roleplay, not a separate tool: first you sit in the specific buyer's chair, then you cross-examine the draft like a skeptical senior closer. Show the scoring only when the user asked for a rating or the reasoning.

## Part 1: sit in the buyer's chair

Using the real conversation, note who this person is, what they said last, and what the deal needs next. Anything you assume about their pressures (busy season, budget worry, burned by freelancers) is a hypothesis: hypotheses may tune the tone, never the copy.

Then answer, honestly, as them:

- Why is this person in my inbox right now - and does that reason hold up from my side?
- What exactly are they asking me to do, and how much effort is it?
- Do I believe every claim in front of me?
- Does anything smell like pressure, a script, or a template?
- Is there a clear, respectful reason to respond? A sensible next step qualifies. If the draft needs me to fear losing something, that is manufactured urgency - rewrite.

## Part 2: cross-examine each line

For every line, two questions: "why is this line here?" and "so what?"

- Would THIS buyer read past the opening?
- Where is the proof behind this claim - and is the proof real?
- Is the timing honestly motivated?
- Does the ask match the stage, or is it too big or too small for this moment?
- Is this line only here to make the sender feel better?

A line with no answer is cut or rewritten.

## Part 3: score out of 10

- 1.5 - The opening earns attention: specific to this person, this moment.
- 1.5 - Value arrives before any ask.
- 1.0 - The ask suits the stage (small step early; payment on a collection message; essential inputs on onboarding).
- 1.0 - The message does the right job for where the deal stands.
- 1.0 - No wasted lines.
- 1.0 - Their language and register ("just checking in" and "circling back" are automatic deductions).
- 1.0 - Fully truthful: no invented proof, fake scarcity, exaggeration, or unauthorized free work. One invented claim fails the whole draft.
- 1.0 - The closing note fits the stage.
- 1.0 - Tone matches the relationship's temperature.

The pass band is 8.5 and up. Anything lower does not ship.

## Score caps (apply before adding up)

- More than one unrelated ask: ceiling 7.
- Repeats terms already sent, adding nothing new: ceiling 6.
- An opener that could go to any prospect: ceiling 7.5.
- Guilt, pressure, or manufactured urgency: ceiling 6.
- Invents a fact, or moves price, advance, scope, or timelines without the owner's say-so: automatic fail.
- Promises free work or samples the owner never authorized: automatic fail.

## Part 4: repair or stop

Under 8.5? Fix the weakest lines first and run the whole review again - three rounds at most. Still under? Do not dress up a failing draft as a pass. Either ask the user the blocking question (usually a fact only they have, like a real delivery date) or state the honest limitation.

## The anti-inflation rule

Scores never get rounded up, and never rise because a high number would be more comfortable. A 7.5 that is "good enough to send" is reported as 7.5, with its reason, whenever the user asked for the rating. The gate only works if the number can be trusted.
