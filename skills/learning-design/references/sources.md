# Source register - learning-design

Every entry below was directly consulted for this register (pages fetched and read, 6 November 2026). Summaries are written from the source itself. Verify a source is still live before quoting it in a load-bearing context.

## Strong evidence

1. Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques", Psychological Science in the Public Interest. Consulted via the American Educator practitioner summary ("Strengthening the Student Toolbox"): practice testing and distributed practice are the techniques the review rates highest; rereading and highlighting rate low as student study techniques.
   Paper: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266
   Summary consulted: https://elar.urfu.ru/bitstream/10995/42269/1/dunlosky_2013.pdf

2. Rawson, Dunlosky & Sciartelli (2013), "The Power of Successive Relearning", Educational Psychology Review. Consulted abstract: combining retrieval practice with spaced study ("successive relearning") is the proposed technique; the paper reports experiments establishing it in an authentic educational context.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-013-9240-4

3. Worked-example research, Educational Psychology Review special issue (2010). Consulted text: "for novice learners, worked examples are more effective in facilitating learning and transfer compared to conventional problem solving" - the advantage known as the worked-example effect.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9145-4
   Background reading: Sweller (2006), "The worked example effect and human cognition", Learning and Instruction: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2006.02.005

4. "Pathways to Student Motivation" meta-analysis (self-determination theory). Consulted text: conducted over 144 studies and more than 79,000 students; "competence was the strongest predictor of self-determined motivation followed by autonomy, whereas relatedness had only a small" unique contribution.
   https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8935530/

5. SDT-based education interventions meta-analysis (2024). Consulted text: 137 effect sizes, N = 9,433 participants; interventions supporting autonomy and competence show significant positive effects across study designs.
   https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/2024_WangWangEtAl_MetaEdu.pdf

6. "Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning: a Systematic Review of Applied Research in Schools and Classrooms". Consulted text: the authors "screened nearly 2,000 abstracts and systematically coded 50 experiments" (drawn from 37 studies) and find retrieval practice benefits learning across classroom contexts.
   https://remix.berklee.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1008&context=faculty-works

7. "A Meta-analytic Review of the Effectiveness of Spacing and Retrieval Practice for Mathematics Learning", Educational Psychology Review (abstract consulted; published online 29 July 2025). Finds a robust small-to-medium effect of spaced versus massed practice for mathematics (g = 0.28 over 27 studies), while the testing-versus-restudy effect (g = 0.18) was not robust - the authors conclude the literature does not yet provide conclusive evidence for retrieval practice in mathematics.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10035-1

8. PREPRINT (label as preprint if cited): "Retrieval practice versus elaborative encoding: a systematic and meta-analytic review", OSF Preprints (page consulted).
   https://osf.io/4xszh

## Moderate / promising

9. Sailer & Homner (2019), "The Gamification of Learning: a Meta-analysis", Educational Psychology Review. Consulted text reports effect sizes of g = .49 on cognitive, g = .36 on motivational, and g = .25 on behavioral learning outcomes.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w

10. Gamification in education meta-analysis (2023, PMC). Consulted text: overall effect size g = 0.822 (95% CI [0.567-1.078]) - a large average effect; the paper itself notes the diversity of gamification designs and calls for moderator caution. Treat as mixed.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591086/

11. Bjork & Bjork (2011), "Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way: creating desirable difficulties to enhance learning" (chapter PDF consulted): difficulties that trigger encoding and retrieval processes improve long-term learning; difficulties must fit the learner's current level to be "desirable" rather than just hard.
    https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf

## Myths

12. Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008), "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence", Psychological Science in the Public Interest (full text consulted). The review defines the "meshing hypothesis" (instruction matched to a preferred style works better), sets the experimental criteria a valid demonstration would need, and concludes the literature shows a "virtual absence of positive findings" meeting them - resources are better spent on practices with a strong evidence base.
    https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x

## Attention: myth vs measured reality

13. The "8-second attention span" debunking (journalistic verification with expert quotes, consulted). The 8-second figure traces to a Microsoft Canada consumer-insights marketing report; the authors found no peer-reviewed source for it, and attention researchers note decades of research never quantified a universal span.
    https://internet.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/532-Master/532-UnitPages/Unit-09/Goldfish_MythBusting.pdf
    BBC (consulted): experts call the idea of an "average attention span" pretty meaningless and task-dependent: https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790

14. Gloria Mark (UC Irvine), in-situ attention measurement (her site consulted): her multi-year tracking "shows that our attention spans are declining, averaging just 47 seconds on any screen" - a field measurement of screen-switching in her observed samples, not a universal limit.
    https://gloriamark.com/attention-span/

15. Ophir, Nass & Wagner (2009), PNAS, "Cognitive control in media multitaskers" (consulted): heavy media multitaskers are more susceptible to interference from irrelevant stimuli than light multitaskers on cognitive-control tasks.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2747164/

## Product implementation references (Duolingo)

16. Settles (2016), "How we learn how you learn" (consulted): Duolingo's student model uses forgetting curves and a statistical method they call half-life regression to schedule practice.
    https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn/
17. Duolingo teaching method page (consulted): exercises start with simpler material and lead to harder; hints and difficulty adaptation.
    https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-teaching-method/
18. Duolingo research hub: https://research.duolingo.com/

## Ethics / dark patterns

19. "A Systematic Review of User Experiments on the Effects of Dark Patterns", Proceedings of CHI 2026 (page and abstract consulted, published 13 April 2026): experimental studies agree that dark patterns "do significantly alter user behavior (with large variance in effect size)" and that external interventions against them have been mostly unsuccessful.
    https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772318.3790383
