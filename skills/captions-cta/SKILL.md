# Captions & CTA

The visual earns the tap; the caption's first line earns the read. Treat the caption as the second hook, never as a description of the post. And decide the single action the post wants - because a post gets exactly one ask.

## Practitioner rules of thumb (test them; they are not laws)

- Stacking several asks dilutes the response - one CTA per post (https://socialk.it/en/blog/instagram-cta-strategy).
- Questions earn comments when the answer fits in one line (https://later.com/blog/how-to-increase-instagram-engagement/).
- The caption moves the reader through stop, read, respond, click - in that order (https://www.shopify.com/blog/instagram-captions).

## Anatomy of a caption

1. **Line one** (everything above the "more" fold): hook-adjacent to the cover but never a copy of it - add fresh tension or a new specific.
2. **The useful middle**: what would not fit on the visual - full prompts in copy-paste blocks, the steps, the short rules list. Slides hold the lesson; the caption is the workbook.
3. **The honesty line, when needed**: "AI gets things wrong - cross-check." / "Templates, not guarantees."
4. **The single CTA**, picked from the post's goal (next section).
5. **Credits**: when you cite claims or techniques, name the exact sources.
6. **Hashtags**: a small relevant set. Thirty tags is spam.

Write in the creator's actual voice and the audience's actual language - short clauses, spoken rhythm, raw text ready to paste.

## Picking the CTA

- Post teaches -> ask for the save, and name the future moment it pays off.
- Post shares an opinion or story -> ask one easy question, answerable in a line.
- Post names a shared pain -> ask for the share ("send this to the friend who...").
- Post connects to your product -> one link CTA. How often product CTAs appear is a dial you tune from results, not a quota.

## A rewrite, worked

- BEFORE: "Save this post, share it with your friends, comment your favorite tip, and check the link in bio!" - four asks; attention splits four ways and the action the post actually wants competes with the rest.
- AFTER (goal: saves): "Save this - it pays off in exam week." One ask, plus the moment it pays off.
- Same post, different goal (comments): "Which tip are you trying first? Drop the number." Still one ask, now answerable in three seconds.

## Review checklist (score out of 100)

- Line one works as a hook by itself (25)
- The middle is genuinely usable material (20)
- One CTA, and it matches the post's goal (25)
- Honesty and source lines included where needed (15)
- Reads like the creator speaking (15)
