# Creator Analysis Method

## Purpose and limits

The goal of studying a creator is to walk away with STRUCTURE: hook shapes, format choices, pacing, sourcing habits. You do not take their words, their claims, their artwork, or their audience. Every study is dated, cites its observable evidence, and keeps a hard line between what you saw and what you concluded. Nobody gets called a "top performer" without a metric, and every metric is labeled: public count you saw yourself, or their own claim, attributed.

## The breakdown procedure

1. **Verify identity.** Exact handle, real name, current role - from a source less than about six months old, with spelling confirmed on their official site or profile.
2. **Catalog openings.** Take the first line of their ten most recent public posts and classify each against your hook patterns. Which one dominates?
3. **Map the formats.** Short vs long, face-led vs b-roll, carousel vs reel vs story, posting cadence.
4. **Measure value density.** Does a post teach something usable by itself, or only tease a course? What exactly would a viewer save?
5. **Check sourcing habits.** Do they say where their news and claims come from? Adopt the habit; never adopt the claims.
6. **Log shared material.** Public prompts or templates may be referenced WITH credit and a link. Rewording them into your own is theft.
7. **Record performance evidence.** Only counts you can see publicly, or numbers they state themselves - attributed. No labels without one of the two.
8. **Choose the takeaway.** One or two structural moves to test in your own voice. Everything identity-specific stays behind.

## Evidence rules

- Every entry points at a dated, specific artifact: a post, a talk, a page, with a link.
- "Observable" means what is directly visible - a title, a stated method, a view count at check time. "Inference" means your theory about why it works. Label both, always.
- Numbers move. Re-check view and follower counts on the day you use them.
- Scale claims - audience size, revenue, views-per-month - are the creator's words, or a case study's words. Attribute them. A case study published by the agency that sold the work is self-promotion; treat its numbers as claims.
- No video watched, no pacing claim. A title and description support observations about titles and descriptions only; mark anything deeper as pending until you have actually watched.

## Entry template

```
## <Name> (<lane>)
Verified identity: <role, per a source dated within ~6 months, link>.
Observable (<dated source>): <what is directly visible - title, stated method, public metrics at check time>.
Inference (labeled): <your structural reading of why it works>.
Application: <one move to test in your own content>.
```

## Boundaries

- Research means monitoring public content. No mass-following, no engagement pods, no automated interaction with anyone's account.
- No invented affiliations, endorsements, or quotes. Paywalled material stays paywalled.

## Review checklist (score out of 100)

- Identity confirmed on a current source (20)
- Specific dated evidence with links (25)
- Observation and inference kept apart (20)
- Their words and numbers attributed; nothing invented (15)
- Ends with usable takeaways for your next batch (20)
