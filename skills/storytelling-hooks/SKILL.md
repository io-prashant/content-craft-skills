# Storytelling & Hooks

A feed gives you a moment. The opening line, the first slide, the first second and a half - that is where the decision to stay or scroll happens. So judge every beat by one question: does it earn the beat after it? And treat every hook as a promise the rest of the piece must keep. A broken promise is clickbait, and clickbait spends trust.

## Frameworks worth knowing (named sources)

1. **Let the audience do some work.** Andrew Stanton (writer/director of Toy Story and WALL-E), TED 2012: hand them 2+2 instead of 4 - people want to connect the dots themselves. His other rule: decide the ending first, then build every beat toward it. https://www.ted.com/talks/andrew_stanton_the_clues_to_a_great_story
2. **Bank one moment a day.** Matthew Dicks' "Homework for Life" habit: each evening, jot down the day's most story-worthy moment in a sentence or two. Honest small moments outperform manufactured drama, and the habit means you never run out of material. https://matthewdicks.com/homework-for-life/
3. **Pick a shape first.** Kurt Vonnegut's "Shapes of Stories": a story is fortune rising and falling, and the shape (man in hole, boy meets girl, Cinderella) tells the audience where they are. Choose the shape before drafting. https://ed.ted.com/best_of_web/ZG7Q2obA
4. **The Story Spine**, an 8-sentence skeleton created by improviser Kenn Adams in the late 1980s (NPR interview, June 2026: https://www.npr.org/2026/06/23/nx-s1-5750619/meet-the-creator-of-the-story-spine-an-8-sentence-tool-to-create-and-analyze-stories). Rebecca Stockley carried it from his workshop to Pixar's writers; Emma Coats spread it publicly in 2011 inside her tweeted "22 rules of storytelling". Credit Adams - never call it "Pixar's official method". The starters: Once upon a time... Every day... But one day... Because of that... Because of that... Because of that... Until finally... Ever since then...
5. **Why any of this works**, Stanton again: a story's job is to make the audience care. The rest is mechanics.

## Opening patterns to test

Candidates collected from current hook craft writing (content.game, buffer.com/resources/good-hooks, socialk.it). None is a guaranteed winner - test them on your audience and keep the ones your own numbers confirm.

1. **Pain callout** - say the exact hurt the viewer is feeling right now.
2. **Contrarian take** - push against something the audience believes.
3. **Curiosity gap** - open a loop that closes later, without inventing secrets.
4. **Number plus outcome** - concrete and bounded, and the number must be real.
5. **Real stakes** - an actual deadline or cost that is true for the viewer.
6. **POV address** - "You", "POV:" - drops the viewer inside the scene.

## Patterns to refuse

Made-up secrets ("toppers secretly do this" - you cannot know), invented statistics, income bait, "guaranteed" outcomes, virality borrowed from someone else's results.

## Craft rules for openings

- The first line has to land with the sound off: cover text, first frame, caption line one.
- One idea per opening. Delete every word that pulls no weight.
- Draft in the audience's real spoken language and rhythm, then read it aloud. If it does not sound like a person talking, rewrite it.
- Whatever the opening promises, the final slide or second delivers. Loop opened, loop closed.
- Say the topic plainly on the cover. Tension is fine; hiding the subject is not.

## A rewrite, worked

Applying Made to Stick's Simple + Unexpected + Concrete (https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/books/made-stick-why-some-ideas-survive-others-die):

- BEFORE: "AI tools for students you should know" - a topic label, no stakes, no curiosity.
- AFTER: "Exam in 7 days? 5 AI prompts that change how you study." - one idea (Simple), a countdown instead of a listicle (Unexpected), 7 days and 5 prompts (Concrete). The viewer knows the payoff and why it matters now.

## Building a carousel as a story

Each slide has to justify the next swipe:

1. **Cover**: the hook - a bold, plain promise. Never a logo or brand opener.
2. **Second slide**: why this matters to the viewer right now.
3. **Middle**: one idea per slide, each more useful or more specific than the one before. Mid-story loops are allowed only when truthful - if you say the best tip is on slide 5, it had better be.
4. **Payoff slide**: the full method or result they can use today.
5. **Final slide**: a single ask - save, share, comment, or link. One only.

When a draft feels flat, story-doctor it: lay the draft over the Story Spine, one slide per starter sentence, and find the missing one. It is usually a middle "because of that" - the twist that earns the payoff. Treat that mapping as a structural check, never as evidence of results.

## Building a 20-40 second reel

Personal reflective open, steady escalation, determined close, one invitation. Write for the ear in short clauses, letting breaths punctuate, keeping the creator's natural code-switching. Pair every spoken claim with a visual proof beat.

## The order of a post

Emotion before information:

1. **Spike** - surprise, tension, recognition, or curiosity.
2. **Orientation** - one line on what the viewer will get.
3. **Delivery** - the value, escalating.
4. **Resolution** - the payoff plus a single CTA.

A spike that never resolves into substance is clickbait. Cut it.

## Reading list

- Storyworthy - Matthew Dicks: the moment-collection habit and the "five-second moment".
- Made to Stick - Chip & Dan Heath: the SUCCESs checklist (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories).
- Building a StoryBrand - Donald Miller: the audience is the hero; the brand is the guide. https://storybrand.com/downloads/your-brand-is-not-the-hero.pdf
- The Anatomy of Story - John Truby; Story - Robert McKee: long-form structure.
- Save the Cat - Blake Snyder: beat pacing for short scripts.

## Pre-publish checklist

Score each item 0-20, total out of 100:

- One read tells the viewer the topic and the payoff
- The curiosity loop is honest - nothing invented
- The opening is specific enough that only you could have written it
- Each beat makes the next one feel necessary
- The ending keeps the opening's promise, with one honest CTA

Under 80? Fix the opening before touching anything else.
