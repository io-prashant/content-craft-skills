# Studio Brand-Content Playbook

## Who you are talking to

A creator account performs for fans; a studio account reassures buyers. The person scrolling is a founder or marketer quietly asking: "Can this person make my brand look good - and can I trust them with it?" Every post exists to answer that.

## Positioning principles

- Sell the feeling, not the service list. What clients buy is a story their audience connects with; the deliverables are how you get there.
- Being small is a feature. One person taking a project from idea to first cut is a selling point - so never inflate yourself into a roster, a team, or a client list that does not exist.

## Four pillars

1. **Proof of craft.** Finished work and designed pieces; real footage frames over plain text. Each post carries its own visual concept, and each post shows one concrete example or teaches one usable brand lesson. A generic "we make ads" claim card with neither is not a post.
2. **Teach the buyer.** Break down a hook, fix a script before/after, explain why one frame works. Demonstrating thinking earns trust faster than declaring skill.
3. **An honest journey.** Build in public using only what genuinely happened. No invented wins, clients, revenue, or momentum.
4. **Open process.** Show how a brief turns into an ad - mining the brand and its competitors for ideas, scripting, production, edit, feedback - described as method, not as tool marketing.

## Non-negotiables

- No invented statistics, no unnamed "big clients", no "#1", no delivery-time promises, no prices in posts, no sweeping universal claims.
- Spec work is labeled as spec work. Concept pieces are never dressed up as paid campaigns - that honesty is itself part of the brand.
- Better one strong post than five fillers. Never publish generic content to hit a number.
- Journey and claim posts draw only on real events and real work.

## Formats that work

- Full-bleed footage frame with a gradient and a confident headline.
- Typographic card anchored by a real frame - only when the line itself is a lesson or insight worth a card.
- Reels shot phone-first at 9:16 and previewed at phone size. Retire any format that assumes a desktop viewer.

## Captions for buyers

Structure: proof, then process, then exactly one CTA - a DM keyword, a direct message link, or a single question a founder would genuinely answer. Pricing never appears in captions; it belongs in the DM conversation. Standard caption rules still hold: the first line works as its own hook, and there is only ever one ask.

## Studying other people's ad work

Keep a reference file, and hold every entry to the same standard: a dated, specific ad or post with a link; metrics split into what is publicly visible versus what the creator claims (attributed); the visible craft mechanism; and one testable idea for your own grid. View counts are not evidence of deal flow, and an agency's case study about its own client is a sales document - quote it as a claim, never as a result.

## Review checklist (score out of 100)

- A founder would stop scrolling and feel trust (25)
- Shows real work or teaches a real lesson instead of claiming (25)
- Consistent with the positioning in your bio and site (15)
- Every non-negotiable respected (20)
- Exactly one CTA, appropriate for a buyer (10)
- The visual concept is its own idea, not a re-skin (5)
